02

Train, then test

Period 1 · 7 minutes · the lesson

Every AI is made in three steps. Engineers use these exact words.

Step 1

Examples

Lots of labelled examples: "this is a happy face", "this is a sad face". Engineers call this training data.

→
Step 2

Training

The computer looks for patterns in the examples. The result is called a model.

→
Step 3

Testing

Give the model new examples it never saw, and count how often it is right.

Why new examples?

Your teacher gives you the exact exam questions the day before. You memorise the answers and get 100%. Do you really understand the lesson?

Maybe not. A real test uses new questions. AI is the same: we test it on examples it has never seen, or the score lies.

    05

    When AI is unfair

    Period 2 · 7 minutes · the lesson

    An AI learned "apple" from these examples only. Now we show it a green apple.

    Training examples
    The AI says

    Waiting for a test…

    This is called bias

    When the examples are one-sided, the AI is unfair to everything it did not see.

    It happens in real life. In 2018, researchers tested face programs from big companies: they made under 1% mistakes on light-skinned men, and up to 35% on dark-skinned women, because the examples were mostly of light-skinned men. (Gender Shades study)
    06

    Mission: trick the AI

    Period 2 · 15 minutes · teach it lies, then fix it

    This is a brand new AI. Its brain is empty. You will teach it that circles are squares and squares are circles. What will it believe?

      What just happened

      The AI is not clever or stupid. It believes whatever its examples say. Wrong examples make a wrong AI.

      Engineers say: garbage in, garbage out. Real AIs learn from millions of examples written by people. If people made mistakes, the AI learns the mistakes too.
      07

      Mission: look inside the brain

      Period 2 · 13 minutes · a real neural network

      Open TensorFlow Playground (by Google, free, no account). It shows a real neural network learning, live.

        How to read it
        • Circles are neurons.
        • Lines are weights: how much one neuron listens to another.
        • Thick line = listens a lot. Thin = hardly listens.
        • Colour shows if it pushes the answer one way or the other.
        • Learning = the computer changing the weights, again and again, until the mistakes go down.
